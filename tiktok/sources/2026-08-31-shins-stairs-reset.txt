If stairs make your shins burn, start with this 30-second reset

If your shins light up halfway up the stairs, do not start by stretching your calves harder. Try this first: stand with one foot on the bottom step. Keep your heel down. Slowly let your knee travel forward over the middle toes, then return. Do five calm reps on each side.

Why that tiny movement? Going upstairs asks your ankle to bend while the muscles on the front of your lower leg control the shin. After hours at a desk, many people go straight from a fixed ankle angle to demanding stairs. The front of the lower leg has to work suddenly, and it can feel like it is doing all the work.

Imagine leaving the office, taking the station stairs two at a time, and feeling that familiar hot pull before you even reach the first landing. That does not automatically mean something is damaged. It can be your lower leg asking for a smoother handoff.

With PREVIA, you can test how each ankle and leg handles that movement instead of guessing. Look for one side that cannot let the knee move forward as easily, or a foot that collapses inward while you try.

Then make stairs less of a surprise. Before a walk or commute, use the step reset for 30 seconds. On the stairs, slow down for the first few steps and let the whole foot stay connected. Add a short walk break during the day if you have been sitting for hours.

This is not a diagnosis, and sharp pain, swelling, or pain that keeps getting worse deserves proper medical assessment. But for that desk-to-stairs burning feeling, your first move may be preparation, not more aggressive stretching.
