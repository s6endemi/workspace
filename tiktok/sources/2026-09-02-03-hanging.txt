You did not lose the ability to hang. Desks took it.

Try this for one second. Reach both arms overhead as if you were grabbing a branch. Does your chest pop up, your ribs fly forward, or do your shoulders feel like they run out of room before your hands get there?

That is not an age problem. It is a use problem.

As kids, we hang from playground bars without planning it. We reach, swing, climb, and let our shoulders move around the ribcage. Then the adult schedule arrives. Keyboard. Phone. Steering wheel. Sofa. Your arms spend most of the day in front of you, and overhead becomes a position you only visit when you need something from the top shelf.

Your body gets efficient at the positions it repeats. It does not erase the old movement. It stops keeping it ready.

With PREVIA, you can test this yourself in a few minutes. The movement scan can show whether your shoulders, ribs, and upper back are sharing an overhead reach or whether one area is doing all the compensating.

Bring it back gently. Stand at a wall and slide both arms upward six times, keeping your ribs quiet. Hold a doorway with both hands and take three slow steps back, then breathe for 20 seconds. If a bar is available, hang with your feet still touching the floor for 15 seconds, twice. Let the shoulders move. Do not turn it into a strength contest.

The goal is not to prove you can hang like a kid. It is to give your shoulders a movement they used to get for free.

The top shelf should not feel like a special event. Your body remembers more than your workday lets it show.
