Why your ribs feel stiff when you reach for the top shelf

You reach for a mug on the top shelf and your arm goes up, but your ribs feel like they are stuck in a jacket two sizes too small. Most people blame the shoulder. Sometimes the shoulder is only where you notice the problem.

For an arm to reach overhead, the shoulder blade has to rotate and your rib cage has to move with it. If your day has been one long sequence of rounded laptop posture, shallow breaths, and shoulders held near your ears, that motion can feel unfamiliar by dinner. Your arm keeps reaching, while the rest of the system stays braced.

Picture the kitchen scene: one hand holds a pan, the other reaches for a spice jar, and there is that weird tug along the side of your chest. It is easy to force the arm higher. A better first check is to exhale gently, let your ribs soften, and reach again without shrugging.

With PREVIA, you can test whether one side of your upper body moves differently when you lift your arms. It is a quick way to see the pattern rather than treating every tight feeling as a shoulder problem.

Try this after a long desk block: place your forearms on a wall at shoulder height. Breathe out slowly, then slide the arms upward only as far as you can without pinching or hiking the shoulders. Three relaxed repetitions are enough to notice whether the movement changes.

Do not push through sharp pain, tingling, breathing trouble, or symptoms that do not settle. This is about giving your ribs and shoulder blade a chance to join the reach again, not forcing a bigger range of motion.
