Your knees may be doing a job your hips stopped doing

You notice it when you walk downstairs. A small crackle in one knee. That hot, tired feeling after a long queue. The moment you stand up from a low sofa and your knees complain before the rest of you has even moved.

Most people stare straight at the knee. That makes sense. It is where the noise and the annoyance show up.

But the knee sits between two big joints. If the ankle does not bend smoothly and the hip does not help guide the leg, the knee often becomes the middle manager for a problem that started somewhere else. It twists, steadies, and absorbs while the joints above and below it stay quiet.

Picture a door hinge with a frame that keeps shifting. You can oil the hinge all day. The hinge is still working inside a crooked frame.

With PREVIA, you can test this yourself in a few minutes. The movement scan can show whether one ankle, hip, or side of your body is making the knee take more of the job.

Start small. At a wall, send one knee slowly toward the wall without lifting the heel. Do eight gentle repetitions per side. Then stand next to a chair, sit back for five slow sit-to-stands, and notice whether both feet push the floor evenly. Finish with 20 seconds of standing on one leg beside a wall, lightly touching it if needed.

You are not trying to bully your knees into silence. You are giving the rest of the chain a chance to show up.

For many people, the first change is simply noticing that one side has been doing all the negotiating. That is useful information, and it is a lot more interesting than blaming a noisy knee.
