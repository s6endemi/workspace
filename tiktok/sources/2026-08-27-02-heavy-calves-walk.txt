Why your calves feel heavy on an easy walk

Your calves can feel done before the walk has really started, even on flat ground.

It often shows up halfway through an ordinary walk. Your feet start searching for a comfortable position. One calf feels tighter than the other. You slow down at a crossing, and the first few steps after standing still feel oddly clunky. You may assume you need tougher calves. Sometimes the more interesting part is lower down.

Every step asks your foot to do more than land. Your heel touches down, your foot adapts to the ground, your big toe helps you leave it again, and your calf passes force through that chain. When the foot stays stiff or the big toe barely joins the push-off, the calf can end up doing extra work on every single step. Nothing dramatic happens. It is just hundreds of small handovers that never get very smooth.

Before your next walk, stand barefoot or in socks near a wall. Keep your heel down and let one knee travel gently forward over the middle toes for 30 seconds. Then stand with both feet flat and slowly lift all ten toes, lower them, and press just the big toes into the floor five times. For the first minute of the walk, shorten your stride slightly and notice whether you can roll through the whole foot instead of pushing off the outside edge.

This is not a test of how strong you are. It is a reminder that the foot and calf are supposed to share the job.
