It Is Not The Heavy Things That Catch Your Back Out

It is not the heavy things that catch your back out. It is the ones you did not know were heavy.

Picture yourself in the hallway, grabbing the crate of empty bottles by the door and swinging it up in one quick careless motion, when somebody has already refilled it: everything tightens a fraction of a second too late, and you put it straight back down. Now picture the same crate, the same bottles, except this time you know: you set your feet, take a breath, and it comes up without a word.

Your body gets ready for a weight before it ever takes it, but only for a weight it knows about.

That is why a shopping bag with a forgotten tin of paint in it can go worse than the packed suitcase you carried down two flights yourself. It is why catching a bag of dog food that somebody tosses you costs you more than carrying it the whole way home. And here is the part that surprises people: your back handles far more than you think, as long as it gets the half second it needs.

So before you pick up anything you have not picked up before, nudge it with one hand first and let the weight tell you what it is.
