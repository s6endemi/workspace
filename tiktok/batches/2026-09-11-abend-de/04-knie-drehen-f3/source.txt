A Knee That Catches When You Turn Is Not A Weak Knee

A knee that catches when you turn is not a weak knee. It is a knee that almost never gets to turn.

Picture yourself at the kitchen counter with both feet planted, reaching around for a mug in the cupboard behind you: your feet stay where they are, your body keeps turning, and halfway round the knee gives a small sharp nudge that stops you. Now do it again and step one foot around first, and the whole turn happens in one easy motion, nothing catching at all.

Most of the day asks that knee to go forward and back and nothing else, walking, stairs, sitting down, standing up, so the small turn inside it goes months without being asked for.

That is why closing a patio door with a quick flick of your foot can feel oddly risky, while a long walk in a straight line never does. It is why twenty minutes of dancing at a wedding asks more of your legs than a whole day of walking around town. And here is the part that surprises people: a knee that turns a little, often and on purpose, ends up feeling steadier than one that is simply strong.

So once a day, stand on one leg, hold on to a chair if you like, and turn your upper body slowly left and right ten times, letting the knee turn a little with you.
