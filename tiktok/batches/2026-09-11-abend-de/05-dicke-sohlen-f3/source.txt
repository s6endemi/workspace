Thick Soft Soles Do Not Protect Your Feet

Thick, softly padded soles do not protect your feet. They quietly switch them off.

Picture yourself in a furniture shop, testing a very soft mattress: you climb onto it in your socks, lift one foot to see how it feels, and within two seconds you are hopping about to stay upright, because the foam gives way wherever you press. Step down onto the hard shop floor and try exactly the same thing, and you can stand there on one leg, calm and still, for as long as you like.

Your foot reads the ground through the skin underneath it, and every reading turns into a small correction that keeps you upright. Put five centimetres of soft foam in between and most of those readings never arrive, so the corrections get lazy.

That is why the last steps down a dark staircase are the ones you slow right down for, one hand on the rail. It is why stepping off a kerb in the dark makes you plant your foot far more carefully than you used to. And here is the part that surprises people: a firmer, thinner sole usually leaves you steadier, not less steady.

So a few times a week, walk around your home barefoot for ten minutes on purpose, over a rug, over tiles and over the doormat, all in the same walk.
