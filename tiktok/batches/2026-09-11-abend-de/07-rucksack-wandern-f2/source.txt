Why Your Backpack Still Pulls You Forward Long After You Take It Off

If you are hiking with a full backpack this weekend, stay with me for a second. Three things, and they change how you stand once you finally set it down.

Here's the mechanic: a full pack hangs behind you, and you answer it the only way you can, by leaning forward until it feels like it is sitting over your feet again. Picture yourself climbing a steep trail with a full pack, the hip belt hanging open and unused, both straps digging into your shoulders, and with every step you tip a little further forward just to keep from tipping over backward.

First thing: close the hip belt and pull it tight before you touch the shoulder straps again, so the weight settles on your hips instead of your shoulders. Second thing: every twenty minutes, brace the pack against a rock or a railing for a few breaths and let both arms hang and swing loose. If you want more of this, feel free to follow Previa Health.

And the third one, almost nobody does this: when you finally take the pack off back at the car, do not just drop it and walk away. Stand still and consciously straighten up for a few seconds first, because your back keeps that forward lean for a while even after the weight is already gone.

Nothing about your back gave out on that trail. It simply spent hours leaning forward to balance a weight that was never resting where it should, and it needs a moment to remember the other position.
