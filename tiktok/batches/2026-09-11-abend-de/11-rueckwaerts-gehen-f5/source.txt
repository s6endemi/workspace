Walking Backwards Is A Completely Different Skill

You have walked forwards a few hundred million steps in your life. Backwards, maybe a few hundred, and your body puts those together in a completely different way.

Picture yourself carrying one end of a table down your own hallway, walking backwards the whole length of it: your steps get short, your toes touch down before anything else, you go slower and slower, and at some point you glance over your shoulder for a floor you already know by heart.

Forwards, your body runs a pattern it has practised your whole life and never has to think about. Backwards, it has to assemble the same steps in an order it hardly ever uses, and you can feel it working.

And here is the part almost nobody expects: this is the one everyday movement most adults have not done twenty steps of in a year. It shows up when you edge out of a crowded cafe between the chairs, when you back away from a full boot to shut it, and on a playground, where children run backwards for fun and land it every time. Watch anyone walk backwards and you will see the same short, careful steps within seconds, every single time.

So here is the payoff: now and then, on a flat and empty stretch, a quiet pavement, your hallway, the grass beside the path, walk twenty steps backwards, slowly, and let your feet find the ground on their own.
