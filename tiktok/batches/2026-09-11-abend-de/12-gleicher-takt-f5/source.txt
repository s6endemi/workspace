Why Two People Walking Together End Up In The Same Rhythm

Walk beside someone for two minutes and your steps will fall into the same rhythm, and neither of you will have decided anything.

Picture yourself walking down a long pavement next to a friend, deep in a conversation about something else entirely: one of you slows a little, the other stretches a little, and by the corner you are putting your feet down at the same moment, like two people who practised it.

And here is the part almost nobody expects: it is usually the steadier rhythm that takes over, not the faster one. You can watch it happen on a stairwell, where three people end up hitting the same step at the same moment, in a queue that shuffles forward as one body, and in a long corridor where four strangers fall into one pace and nobody finds it odd. Walk beside someone whose rhythm is very different from yours for half an hour, though, and your legs will mention it afterwards, because you spent that walk in someone else's tempo instead of your own.

So here is the payoff: once a day, walk five minutes at exactly your own rhythm, nobody beside you, nothing to match.
