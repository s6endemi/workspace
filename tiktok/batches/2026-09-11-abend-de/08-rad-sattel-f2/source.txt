Why An Hour On A Low Saddle Leaves Your Legs Feeling Short

If you are riding your bike to work tomorrow, stay with me for a second. Three things, and they change how your legs feel when you get off again.

Here's the mechanic: a saddle a few centimetres too low keeps your knee bent at the bottom of every single pedal stroke, so an hour of riding never once lets your leg go all the way long. Picture yourself carrying boxes around a low attic where you cannot stand up straight: for twenty minutes you shuffle about with both knees half bent, and when you finally step out onto the landing and straighten up, it is the best thing you have done all day.

First thing: sit on the saddle, put one heel on the pedal at its lowest point and look at that leg. If there is still a clear bend in it, raise the saddle by about a finger's width and try it again. Second thing: every fifteen minutes, stand up on the pedals for ten easy seconds and let both legs go long. If you want more of this, feel free to follow Previa Health.

And the third one, almost nobody does this: when you get off, before you lock the bike up, stand with both feet flat and let your knees go completely straight for a slow count of five. Your legs just spent an hour going halfway, and they need a moment to find the rest.

Nothing about your legs is stiff here. They simply spent an hour in a small part of what they can do, and never got to the end of it. With Previa Health you can see how this looks in your own body in about two minutes on your phone, free, no app.
