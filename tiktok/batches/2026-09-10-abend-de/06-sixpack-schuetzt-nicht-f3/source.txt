A Six Pack Does Not Actually Protect Your Back

A six pack does not protect your back. A stomach that can hold on for a long time does.

Your body rarely needs one huge burst of core strength, what it actually leans on all day is a low, steady hold that does not quit, while you sit, stand and walk around without ever thinking about it. A stomach trained only for one big effort still leaves your back unguarded for the many hours in between. Picture yourself holding a heavy shop door open for a long line of people filing past you: not one strong shove, but holding it steady, again and again, for minutes, your arm never fully resting until the very last person is through.

That is why someone who can do one impressive lift at the gym can still tweak their back reaching into the dishwasher. It is why your back notices the fortieth minute at a desk more than the one heavy box you carried that morning. And here is the part that surprises people: holding a little effort for a long time protects you more than holding a lot of effort for a second ever will.

So once a day, gently pull your stomach in, not hard, just enough to feel it, and hold that for about three minutes while you do something else, like washing dishes.
