The Real Reason You Hold Your Breath When Lifting Something Heavy

This was never explained to you about the sound you make when you lift something heavy. That sharp little gasp, or holding your breath completely until the box is up, isn't panic and it isn't weakness. It's your body trying to brace itself the only way it knows how, by locking the air inside.

Picture yourself in the kitchen, wrestling with a stuck lid on a jam jar, breath held, face turning red, twisting harder and harder with no luck, until you finally let out one loud breath through your mouth as you twist, and the lid just gives way.

And here's the best part, almost nobody notices this: pushing a stuck shopping cart off a curb, or forcing a bulging suitcase shut by sitting on it, people hold their breath there too, and get lightheaded for a second when they finally let it out. Watch anyone struggling with something heavy and you'll catch them doing this within seconds, every single time.

So here's the payoff: next time you lift something heavy, breathe out on purpose the moment the effort starts, a short audible push of air through your mouth, like blowing out one candle.
