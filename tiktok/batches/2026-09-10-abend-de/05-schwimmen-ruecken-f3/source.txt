Swimming Is Not The Back Training Everyone Says It Is

Swimming is not back training. It is the one hour of your week where your back gets to do almost nothing at all.

In the water you weigh next to nothing, and that is exactly what makes swimming feel so good after a long day. But carrying weight is the thing a back gets better at, and in the pool that job is taken away from you completely. An hour of swimming is a wonderful hour. It is simply not the hour that teaches your body to hold you up. Picture yourself walking out of a lake after an hour in the water: while you are still in it every step is light and floating, then with each step up the shore you get heavier, and by the time you reach the dry sand your legs suddenly feel like they belong to you again.

That is why an hour in the pool can leave you loose in the evening and just as stiff the next morning. It is why people who swim three times a week are surprised that two crates up one flight of stairs still finishes them off. And here is the part that surprises people: your back does not need less weight, it needs weight it can handle often.
