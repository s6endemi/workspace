Why Twenty Minutes Of Overhead Work Costs You A Whole Evening

If you are changing a light fitting, painting a ceiling or clearing out the top shelf, stay with me for a second. Three things, and they change how your back feels afterwards.

Here's the mechanic: to reach higher, almost everybody leans back a little, and then holds that lean for as long as the job takes. Twenty minutes later you are still standing in it, and you never once decided to. Picture yourself in the hallway with a roller, painting the ceiling: with every stroke you lean back a finger's width further to reach the far corner, and when you finally step down you are still leaning like that on the way to the sink.

First thing: bring the work down to you instead of stretching up to it. Stand on a solid step or a small ladder, so that the ceiling is right in front of your hands and not above them. Second thing: work in blocks of about three minutes, then let both arms drop by your sides and take five steps before you carry on. If you want more of this, feel free to follow Previa Health.

And the third one, almost nobody does this: when the job is done, do not sit straight down. Stand still for a few seconds, then bend forward loosely a few times, slow and easy, because that lean is still in you long after the roller is back in the tray.
