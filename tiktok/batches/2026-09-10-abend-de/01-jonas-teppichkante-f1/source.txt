Why Jonas Trips Over The Same Carpet Edge Every Week

This is Jonas. Jonas has a problem: at least once a week he catches his toe on the edge of the rug in his own hallway, the same rug that has been lying there for years.

Here is what is happening: every floor he walks on is flat and predictable, tiles, carpet, asphalt, so his feet only ever need a few millimetres of clearance, and over the years his walk quietly settled on exactly that much. Picture Jonas wading out into a lake on a warm afternoon, water up to his knees: suddenly every single step has to come up high and out of the water, and after twenty steps he notices he is moving in a way he has not moved in months.

That is why the doorstep to his balcony catches him, and why the vacuum cleaner cable gets him every Saturday. It happens again on the kerb outside the bakery, where he clips the edge and plays it off as clumsiness. And here is the part almost nobody notices: it never happens on holiday, where the paths are uneven and his feet come up on every step without him deciding anything.

So if this sounds familiar, you are not clumsy, your feet simply stopped being asked to come up any higher.
