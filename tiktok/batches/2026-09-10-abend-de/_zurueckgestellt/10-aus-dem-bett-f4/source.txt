The Real Reason Getting Out Of Bed Is The Hardest Move Of Your Day

This was never explained to you about getting out of bed. Sitting straight up from lying flat is one of the hardest ways your body can move, and most people make it the first thing they do, after eight hours of not moving at all.

Picture yourself lying in a hammock in a garden, trying to sit straight up out of it: nothing works, you rock a bit and sink back in. Then you swing one leg over the side, roll onto your shoulder, push up on your arm, and you are sitting there without any effort at all.

And here's the best part, almost nobody notices this: on the mornings you oversleep and shoot out of bed sideways in a panic, nothing hurts, because the panic accidentally does it in the right order. The same three movements get you out of a low sofa, and out of a hotel bed that is too high. Watch anyone in their eighties get out of bed and you will see all three, slowly and deliberately, exactly as they were meant to go.

None of this means your back is fragile, it just means one big movement got picked where three small ones were waiting. So here's the payoff: from tomorrow, roll onto your side first, let both legs drop over the edge, and push yourself up with the lower arm as they swing down.
