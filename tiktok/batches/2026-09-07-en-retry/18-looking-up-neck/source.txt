Nobody explains why looking up strains your neck, not your back

Nobody ever explained why you feel it in your neck, not your back, when you look up at a high shelf.

Looking up comes half from the upper back, and after years at a screen the upper back stays rounded, so the neck has to bend the whole way alone. Picture yourself reaching for the top shelf in a thick winter coat zipped up to your chin: the coat holds your upper back curled, so your neck has to bend twice as far before you can even see the shelf.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether it is really your upper back that stopped moving.

You know the signs. Checking the top kitchen cupboard, your chin tips back long before your eyes reach the shelf. Backing the car out, turning to look over your shoulder, only your neck seems to twist. Watching something fly overhead, your neck aches within seconds. And especially the second fix below, almost nobody does it before reaching up, and it is the one that gives your back the movement back.

First, lying on your back, place a rolled towel across your upper back and hold it there for one minute. Second, before you look up, lift your breastbone toward the ceiling first, ten times. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, stand in a doorway and stretch your arms along the frame for thirty seconds. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see how much of the reach your upper back has quietly handed to your neck.

Nothing in your neck is broken, it just kept doing the work your upper back gave up years ago.
