The Real Reason Your Leg Tingles When Sitting

Does your leg tingle or go slightly numb when you sit? Save this video, in a moment I show you the three things that really help. Stay with me, this is quick.

Sit for a long time, and a glute muscle presses down on the nerve that carries feeling to your foot. Picture yourself sitting on your garden hose in the yard while trying to water the flowers: only a trickle reaches the nozzle in your hand, and that trickle is the tingling.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether that pressure is really what is happening on your side.

You know the signs. Your foot tingles first, then it goes slightly numb. Standing up brings a few seconds of pins and needles. One side is almost always worse, and by evening it fades slower. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it, and it is the one that calms the tingling fastest.

First, stand up every thirty minutes and take ten slow steps, even just to the kitchen and back. Second, while sitting, cross one ankle over the opposite knee and lean forward gently for thirty seconds on each side. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, straighten one leg and slowly lift and lower your foot ten times. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which side is really under pressure.

Nothing in your leg is broken. A muscle is simply sitting where it should not, and it is easy to teach it to let go.
