What Your Bouncing Foot Is Actually Trying To Do

This was never explained to you about your own fidgeting. That bouncing foot under the table isn't bad manners, and it isn't restlessness either. It's your body looking for the smallest movement it's still allowed to make when everything else about you has to stay still.

Picture yourself in a two hour meeting, one foot tapping against the leg of the table under your chair, your coffee cup untouched in front of you, because the rest of you isn't allowed to move an inch, and this tiny motion is the only door left open.

And here's the best part, almost nobody notices this: let yourself bounce through a long meeting and you usually stand up less stiff than the person sitting perfectly still next to you. Push the bounce down on purpose, and you get up stiffer, not calmer. Watch any kid at a dinner table, legs swinging under the chair, and you're just watching a body that still says all of this out loud, before it learns to whisper it instead.

So here's the payoff: stop fighting the bounce. Let your foot go, and once an hour, turn it into a real move, stand up and walk twenty steps, that's it.
