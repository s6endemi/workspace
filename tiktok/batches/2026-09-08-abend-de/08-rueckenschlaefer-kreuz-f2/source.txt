Why Sleeping On Your Back Leaves You Sore

If you sleep on your back and your lower back complains every single morning, stay with me for a second. Three things, and they actually change this.

Here's what's happening: lying flat with your legs stretched out leaves a small gap under your lower back, and you hold that gap for eight hours straight without ever knowing it. Picture yourself lying on a wooden bench in the park on a warm afternoon, legs stretched out, a book face down on your chest: after ten minutes you start shifting around, because the small of your back is not resting on anything, and that's exactly what your mattress does every night.

First thing: put a pillow under your knees before you fall asleep. That one small change closes the gap and lets your lower back rest flat all night. Second thing: in the evening, before bed, hold a half kneel for thirty seconds on each side. If you want more of this, feel free to follow Previa Health.

And the third one, almost nobody does this: right before you fall asleep, pull both knees up to your chest ten times and stretch them back out. That small motion resets your lower back for the whole night, like closing a drawer that's been left half open.

Nothing about your back is damaged here. It's just been holding that same small gap for years, one night at a time. With Previa Health you can see how this looks in your own body in about two minutes on your phone, free, no app.
