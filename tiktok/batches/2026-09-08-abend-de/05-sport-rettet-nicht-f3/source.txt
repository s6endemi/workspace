Exercise And Sitting Run On Two Separate Accounts

One hour of exercise does not erase eight hours of sitting. Your body keeps them on two completely separate accounts, and paying into one does not refill the other.

Movement and stillness are not opposites that simply cancel out, your body tracks them apart. Training fills one account: blood flow, movement, muscle activity. Long sitting drains a different account entirely, and no amount of gym time deposits back into that one once the day is already gone. Picture yourself walking through the desert all day, and then, at eight in the evening, drinking one full bottle of water in a single go: it helps, real relief, but it does not undo the eight thirsty hours that came before it.

That is why people who train hard every morning can still feel just as stiff by evening as someone who never exercises at all. It is why your back can still complain at your desk on days you already went to the gym. And here is the part that surprises people: one strong hour in the morning does not make your whole afternoon feel any looser.

So the fix is not more training, it is breaking up the sitting itself: every forty five minutes, stand up for two minutes and move, even just to another room. That goes straight onto the second account, the one training cannot reach from a distance. And take one phone call a day standing up and walking around.
