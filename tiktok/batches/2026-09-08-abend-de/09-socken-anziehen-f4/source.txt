The Real Reason You Sit Down To Put On Socks

This was never explained to you about putting on your socks. Standing there on one leg is a small balance skill, and you use it without ever thinking about it. When you started sitting down for this, you didn't lose that skill. You just quietly stopped using it.

Picture yourself standing in the hallway, sock in one hand, hopping on one leg toward the wall on your third try, while your kid next to you does the exact same thing standing perfectly still, like it's nothing at all.

And here's the best part, almost nobody notices this: first the wall becomes your support, just a fingertip against it. Then it's the chair. Then it's the edge of the bed, and somewhere along the way you stopped noticing you'd moved down the list. The same thing happens tying your shoes. Escalators start to feel wobbly under your feet for no clear reason.

None of this happened overnight, and none of it means anything is wrong with you, it just means balance is a skill you can quietly stop practicing without ever deciding to. So here's the payoff: every morning, put both socks on standing up, one finger on the wall if you need it, no shame in that.
