Why A City Trip Leaves You Looser Than A Weekend

After a week of walking around a new city, your body moves better than after a weekend on the couch, even though you did far more.

That's because your body doesn't log rest as sitting still, it logs rest as variety. Picture yourself walking through a new city all day: up the steps of a cathedral, over uneven cobblestones, through a museum, in and out of a dozen little shops, then falling into bed tired but loose. Now picture the Sunday afterward, the one spent on the couch with a show, when you finally get up one hand at a time, pushing off the armrest.

The Monday after a couch weekend is usually the stiffest day of the week, even though you barely did anything. The Monday after an active trip, you sit down at your desk and actually feel fine, despite the travel, the packing and the lost sleep. And here's the part almost nobody expects: feeling tired and feeling stiff are two completely different things, and it's entirely possible to come home exhausted and loose at the same time, or rested and stiff at the same time.

So here's what actually helps. Treat your weekend a little like a city trip instead of a reset button. Spend about two hours walking around your own city on foot, taking a route you've never taken before.
