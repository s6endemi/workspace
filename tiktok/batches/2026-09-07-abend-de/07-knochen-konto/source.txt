Your Bones Are Quietly Running A Budget

Your bones are a bank account, and after thirty most people only make withdrawals.

Every bone in your body gets rebuilt all the time, and it gets rebuilt exactly where it feels a pull or a knock, nowhere else. Picture yourself carrying a full crate of water bottles up the stairs once a day: the railing you grab on the way up stays polished and firm, while the railing down the hall that nobody ever touches slowly loosens in its screws. Your skeleton runs on the same logic. It quietly clears out material from every part of you that never gets asked to do anything.

That explains a few strange things. Swimmers, who glide through water with almost no impact, often end up with lighter bones than runners who pound the pavement every morning. A leg locked in a cast can lose in a few weeks what took months of ordinary walking to build. And here is the part almost nobody expects, so stay for this one: twenty small hops on the spot can do more for your bones than a long, easy walk, because a hop lands with a jolt a walk never gives.

So the fix is not more time on your feet, it is more moments where your body actually feels that jolt. Twenty small hops in place some time during your day.
