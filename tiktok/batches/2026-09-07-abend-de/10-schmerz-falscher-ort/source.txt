Why Your Knee Pain Might Start One Joint Higher

Your knee hurts, but the real problem sits one floor higher, and no one has ever told you that. Your body often reports overload where it lands, not where it started. A hip that has stopped turning freely quietly hands its share of the work down to the knee, and the knee ends up sending you the bill for work it never signed up for.

Picture yourself in a shared flat where the neighbour above lets the bath overflow: the water drips through your ceiling, and you spend the evening mopping your own floor instead of ever knocking upstairs. Your knee is the ceiling. The hip is the neighbour who never learned to turn off the tap.

You feel it on the stairs, your knee complaining on every step while your hip barely bends at all. Take your shoes off and you notice your foot aches at the ball whenever your ankle refuses to fold. And especially the third one catches people off guard: a ribcage that will not rotate quietly pushes the whole job onto one shoulder instead.
