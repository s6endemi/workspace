Why You're Taller In The Morning Than At Night

You're a centimeter taller in the morning than you are at night, and the reason changes how you should sit.

Every night, the discs between the bones in your back act like sponges and soak up fluid while you rest. During the day, gravity and sitting squeeze that fluid back out again, and sitting still for hours speeds that up. Picture yourself pressing a wet kitchen sponge flat on the counter all afternoon, then watching it slowly drink itself back to full size on the drying rack overnight.

That's why your car seat can suddenly feel like it's sitting you too high by mid afternoon, even though nothing on it moved. It's also why your lower back often aches more by evening than it does right when you wake up. And here's the one that sounds made up: astronauts really do come back from space measurably taller, because without gravity pulling on them all day, those same discs stay fully soaked the whole time they're up there.

So here's what actually helps. Every forty five minutes, stand up and walk about twenty steps, just enough to shift the load off your back.
