Why Muscle Soreness Waits Two Full Days

Sore muscles don't show up the next day. They show up the day after that, and there's a reason for it.

Tiny tears appear in your muscle the moment you push hard, but the real repair work, with swelling and extra sensitivity, doesn't ramp up until about a day or two later, once your body has actually noticed the damage and sent help. Picture yourself scraping a knee on the pavement: it barely stings while you keep playing, and only the next morning, once the repair crew has fully moved in, does it start to throb.

That's why walking down the stairs the day after hurts more than walking up them, since slowing your body down tears more than lifting it ever does. It's also why the soreness hits harder after a long break than it used to when you trained often, because your body forgot how much rebuilding this movement usually takes. And here's the part almost nobody believes: stretching afterward barely changes any of it, gentle movement does far more.
