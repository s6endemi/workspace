Your neck cracks when you turn your head and this is why (vector test)

Your neck cracks every time you turn your head? Nobody has told you what that really is.

The crack itself is usually harmless, small gas bubbles in the joint fluid, like opening a bottle. The real question is why you have to turn your neck so far in the first place. Below your neck sits your upper back, twelve vertebrae built to rotate. After hours over a screen it stiffens like a rusty door hinge. So every time you look over your shoulder, the neck does the whole turn alone, and it clicks in protest.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether your upper back still turns with you.

You know the signs. You turn your whole body to check the blind spot. Your shoulders sit close to your ears in the evening. A headache starts at the back of your skull. And especially number three of the fixes, nobody expects it, it does not even touch the neck.

First, the towel turn. Roll a towel, put it lengthwise between your shoulder blades on the floor, lie on it and let your arms fall open for one minute. Second, the seated rotation. Sit tall, hands on your ribs, turn your chest left and right five times without moving your hips. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, the eye lead. Look with your eyes first, then let the head follow slowly, ten times each way, so the neck stops rushing ahead of the back.

The crack is not the problem. It is the sound of a neck that works alone. Give the back its turn back.
