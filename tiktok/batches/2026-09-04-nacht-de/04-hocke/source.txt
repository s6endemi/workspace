A toddler can still squat all the way down. When did you stop?

Can you still lower into a full squat, heels flat on the ground? Most adults cannot anymore, and it has nothing to do with age. You did not know that? Then listen very closely.

Watch a toddler play on the floor. Full squat, heels down, spine long, holding that position for ten minutes straight. Somewhere between that age and now, most people lose it, and it is not the knees that changed first, it is the ankles. A toddler never sat in a chair with the ankle locked at ninety degrees, never wore a shoe with a raised heel. You have done both every day for decades, and the ankle joint quietly adapted.

Previa Health shows you in a short test how far your ankle actually bends before your heel lifts off the ground.

You know the signs. Tying your shoes means sitting down first. Picking something off the floor turns into a small production. Getting up from a low couch needs a push from your hands. And especially the third fix, almost nobody does it, and it opens the ankle the fastest.

First, elevate your heels on a book and hold a squat for thirty seconds, knees tracking gently over your toes. Second, face a wall and drive one knee toward it ten times per side, heel staying flat. Follow Previa Health so you do not miss the next body signal. Third, hold a door frame for support and lower into a full squat five times, five seconds each, a little deeper every round.

Nothing about your hips is broken. Chairs and shoes just never asked your ankle to bend again.
