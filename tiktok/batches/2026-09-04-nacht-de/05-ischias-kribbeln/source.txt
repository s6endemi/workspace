Your leg tingles or goes slightly numb while you sit? Save this video, because in a moment I show you the three things that actually help. You did not know that? Then listen very closely.

A long nerve runs from your lower back down the back of your leg, and for part of that path it passes right underneath one of your glute muscles. Sit for a long time, especially with a wallet in your back pocket or one leg crossed, and that muscle presses on the nerve. Picture sitting on a garden hose. Water still trickles through, so your foot does not go fully dead, but the flow is not clean, and that faint dripping is the tingling you feel.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether that pressure point is more active on one side of your body.

You know the signs. Your foot tingles first, then goes slightly numb. Standing up feels like pins and needles for a few seconds. One side is almost always worse, and by evening it fades slower. And especially the third fix, almost nobody knows it, and it resets the nerve.

First, stand up every thirty minutes and take ten slow steps, even to the kitchen and back. Second, sit tall, cross one ankle over the opposite knee, and lean forward gently for thirty seconds per side. Follow Previa Health so you do not miss the next body signal. Third, extend one leg straight and slowly flex and point your foot ten times, letting the nerve glide.

With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which side carries more pressure.

Nothing in your leg is broken. A muscle is sitting on a hose, and it is easy to teach it to let go.
