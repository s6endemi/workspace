A headache that starts at the back of your head is rarely explained properly

A headache that starts at the back of your head and pulls forward toward your temple is rarely explained properly. You did not know that? Then listen very closely.

After long screen days, small muscles at the base of your skull often hold your head forward for hours without a real break. Imagine carrying a bowling ball at the end of a straight, outstretched arm instead of close to your chest. Held far out, even a light weight gets exhausting fast. Your head can work the same way. Tilted forward, it can feel more than twice as heavy to those small muscles, and they often report the strain as pressure toward your temple or your eyes.

For many people the signs show up quietly. The pressure often starts a few hours into a screen day. Rubbing the base of your skull can bring brief relief. Bright light can suddenly feel harder to handle. And especially the third fix, it takes thirty seconds and almost nobody tries it, and it is often the one that calms things fastest.

First, ten slow chin tucks, drawing your chin straight back like a small double chin, not down. Second, with your fingers, apply gentle steady pressure at the base of your skull for thirty seconds. Follow Previa Health so you do not miss the next body signal. Third, raise your screen to eye level, just once, so your head does not tip forward to see it.

Nothing in your head is broken. For many people it is simply a tired neck, holding a heavy position, asking to be carried differently.
