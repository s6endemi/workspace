One hip simply sits more comfortably turned outward than the other, so your pelvis tips slightly to that side every time you sit down, and your body repeats the comfortable side a thousand times. Picture yourself crossing the lawn on the same worn path every single day, while the grass on the other route grows tall and closes over. The other hip barely gets to move that way, and slowly it forgets how.

You know the pattern. In a meeting, your legs cross themselves before you even notice you did it. On the couch, switching sides feels wrong somehow, almost unstable, like the seat itself resists it. Getting up after a long car ride, one hip feels looser than the other, like it barely worked at all. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it, and it is the one that actually wakes the quiet hip back up.

First, today, consciously cross the other leg three times and notice how different it feels each time. Second, while sitting, rotate both knees outward and then inward, ten times on each side.
