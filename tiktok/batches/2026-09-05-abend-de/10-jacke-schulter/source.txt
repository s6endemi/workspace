Your Second Sleeve Knows What Your Shoulder Forgot

Does your shoulder pinch every time you reach for the second sleeve of your jacket? Nobody ever explained why. Stay with me, this is quick.

To slide your arm into that second sleeve, your shoulder has to rotate back and inward, behind you. But it spends the day rolled forward over a keyboard or a steering wheel, and the front of it slowly shortens. Picture yourself pushing a door whose hinge spring got wound the wrong way round: it opens, just never past halfway, no matter how hard you push.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether your shoulder actually stopped opening all the way.

You know the moments. Reaching the zipper on the back of a dress takes an extra twist that never used to be there. Grabbing something off a high shelf means turning your whole body instead of your arm. Taking a jacket off feels easier than putting it on. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it before they even pick the jacket up, and it is the one that gives the hinge its swing back.

First, stand in a doorway, forearms on the frame, and lean forward gently for thirty seconds, letting the front of the shoulder open. Second, rest the back of one hand on your lower back and slowly push the elbow forward, ten times, no force, just reach. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, roll your shoulder blades backward and down, ten times, like closing a drawer behind you. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which shoulder actually stopped opening.

Nothing in your shoulder is broken. It just spent the whole day being told to stay rolled forward, and it believed you.
