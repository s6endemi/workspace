Standing on one leg, you push your hip out and here is why

Right now, standing on one leg, you push your hip out to the side. Nobody ever explained what that does to your back.

When the muscles on the side of your hip get tired, you hang your pelvis into the joint instead of holding it there yourself, always on the same side, and your lower back bends sideways with it every time. Picture yourself leaning your hip on the same garden gate every time you stop to talk: the gate holds you up, and slowly its hinge gives way on that side.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether it is really one hip that keeps giving way.

You know the signs. Doing the dishes, your weight quietly sinks into one hip without you deciding it. Waiting for the kettle, your hip juts out to the same side again. Standing at the school gate talking to another parent, you lean on the same leg once more. And especially the second fix below, almost nobody does it before the hip gives way again, and it is the one that teaches the hip to hold you instead.

First, stand with your feet hip width apart and consciously keep your weight on both feet for thirty seconds, three times a day. Second, standing, slowly push your pelvis toward the other side and back, ten times. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, hold a side plank against the wall for twenty seconds on each side. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which hip is really doing the holding.
