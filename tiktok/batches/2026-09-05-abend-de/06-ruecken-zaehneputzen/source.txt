Your Back Is Doing The Work Your Hips Should

Does your lower back start pulling the moment you lean over the sink to brush your teeth? Save this video, in a moment I show you the three things that change that right away. Stay with me, this is quick.

That small forward lean at the sink does not come from your hips. It comes from your lower back, holding your whole upper body out in front of you for two minutes. Picture yourself at the sink as a crane: your torso is the boom sticking straight out, the toothbrush is the load at its tip, and there is no chain to share the weight, so the boom carries everything on its own.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether it really is your lower back carrying weight it should not have to.

You know the moments. Washing your face over the sink, that same pull returns. Loading the dishwasher, bending from the waist, your back speaks up. Tying your shoes, you rush just to stand back up. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it in the evening, and it is the one that gives your hips the strength to take the load back.

First, practice the hip hinge in front of a mirror, hips back, spine long, ten times. Second, while you brush your teeth, rest one foot on the tub edge or a small step stool to take the angle off your back. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, in the evening, lie back and lift into a bridge ten times, squeezing your glutes at the top. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see whether your back is doing your hips job.

Nothing in your back is damaged. It just got left holding a job your hips forgot to show up for.
