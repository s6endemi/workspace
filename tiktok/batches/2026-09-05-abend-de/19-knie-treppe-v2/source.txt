Why stairs hurt your knees and what they never told you

Nobody ever explained this part about your knees on the stairs. Stay with me, this is quick.

Your knee is rarely the actual problem, it is the messenger. Picture yourself jumping off a low garden wall with stiff, straight legs instead of bending: the landing slams straight into your knees. Going down every single stair, a hip and an ankle that stopped bending do exactly that to your knee, step after step, and the knee protests loudly while the two stiff joints next to it stay quiet.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether your hip and ankle are still doing their share.

You know the signs. Going down always feels worse than going up. You reach for the railing without even thinking about it. One knee clicks on the fifth step and not on the first. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it, and it is the one that gives the ankle its movement back.

First, go down five steps in slow control, three seconds on each one, feeling your hip and ankle absorb the drop instead of your knee. Second, do ten sit to stands from a chair without using your hands, so your hip learns to carry the load again. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, stretch your calf against a wall for thirty seconds on each side, because a stiff ankle is often the real reason your knee has to catch everything. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which neighbour stopped bending.

Nothing in your knee is broken. It is quietly doing the work of two joints that stopped moving.
