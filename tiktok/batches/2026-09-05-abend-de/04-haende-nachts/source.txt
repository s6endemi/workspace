Your Hands Go Numb At Night For A Reason

Do your hands fall asleep at night, tingling until you have to shake them awake? Save this video, in a moment I show you the three things that change that. Stay with me, this is quick.

At night your wrist folds hard under the pillow and your shoulder rolls forward into the mattress, and together they narrow the path from your neck down into your fingers. Picture yourself lying in bed on top of a garden hose, the hose bent sharply under your shoulder, and the nozzle in your hand giving only a trickle. Water still leaves the tap, but only a dribble reaches the end, and that dribble is the tingling you wake up to.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether that narrowed path is what is waking your hands up at night.

You know the mornings. Your fingers feel thick around the coffee cup handle. Buttoning a shirt takes longer because you cannot quite feel the button. Your phone screen barely registers your thumb until you shake your hand out first. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it before bed, and it is the one that keeps the path open all night.

First, keep your wrist straight at night, do not tuck your hand under the pillow, for three nights in a row. Second, before bed, open your chest in a doorway for thirty seconds, letting your shoulder roll back instead of forward. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, stretch your forearm with your arm fully extended for twenty seconds on each side, right before you turn off the light. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which side is really getting pinched off.

Nothing in your hands is broken. They just spent the night folded around a kink nobody ever pointed out to you.
