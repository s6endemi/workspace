What Standing Up From The Floor Reveals About You

Sit down on the floor, then stand back up without using your hands. Nobody ever explained what that reveals about your body. How many hands did you need? Tell me in the comments.

Standing up from the floor needs your ankle, hip and balance working together at once. Spend years rising only from chairs, sofas and car seats, and that combination goes quiet, not the strength. Picture yourself standing at the bottom of a staircase in your own house that you have not climbed in years, because the elevator is right next to it. The stairs still stand, but the steps feel foreign now, spiderwebs hanging along the railing.

Previa Health shows you in a short test what your movement age is, and whether this combination is the one that went quiet in you.

You know the signs. Sitting on the playground ground and catching your hand on your knee to push up. Being the last one standing after a picnic on the grass. Reaching under the bathroom sink and pulling yourself up by the edge. And after pulling your socks on while sitting on the floor, starting on all fours before standing, especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it, and it brings the whole chain back fastest.

First, sit on the floor two minutes every evening instead of the sofa, switching between cross legged and side sitting. Second, from sitting on the floor, stand up five times using one hand, then five times using none. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, hold a deep squat at a doorframe for thirty seconds, letting your ankles open all the way. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which part went quiet in you.

Nothing in you is broken. The floor just became a stranger, and your body believed you did not need it anymore.
