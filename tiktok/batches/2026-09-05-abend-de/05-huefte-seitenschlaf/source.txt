Why Your Hip Hurts When You Sleep On Your Side

Nobody ever explained why your hip aches when you lie on your side. Stay with me, this is quick.

When you lie on your side, your top leg drifts forward toward the mattress and your hip is left hanging, waiting for the muscles along its outer edge to hold it, muscles that spent the whole day sitting and doing nothing. Picture yourself lying inside a tent whose guy line came loose on one side: the pole still stands, but the loose tarp sags down onto your hip and flaps against it all night.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether it is really that side of your hip that stopped holding its own weight.

You know the mornings. Getting out of bed on that side feels tight before you even stand up. Walking the first steps to the bathroom, that hip drags a little. Standing at the sink, you shift your weight off it without noticing. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it during the day, and it is the one that finally gives those muscles something to do again.

First, put a pillow between your knees every night, so your top leg cannot drift forward and pull the hip out of line. Second, during the day, stand and lift your leg out to the side ten times each side, waking up the muscles that should be holding you. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, while you brush your teeth, stand on one leg for thirty seconds, then switch, so those muscles finally get a job. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which hip is doing the least work.

Nothing in your hip is torn or worn out. It just spent the day sitting still, and the night finally asked it to work.
