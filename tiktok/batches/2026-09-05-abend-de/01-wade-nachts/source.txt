Why your calf cramps the second you fall asleep

Does your calf wake you up in the middle of the night? Save this video, in a moment I show you the three things that stop it from waking you. Stay with me, this is quick.

All day your calf is held short without you noticing. Heels lift it, a chair tucks your foot under you, and at night the blanket points your toes down. Picture yourself holding a rubber band stretched tight between your hands from morning to bedtime. The moment you let go, it does not rest, it snaps into a knot, and that knot is what jolts you awake.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether your calf is really the one staying this short all day.

You know the pattern. Slipping off your heels at night, your calf feels tight. Crossing your legs on the couch tucks the same foot under again. Waking up with your toes pointed like a dancer. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it, and it is the one that keeps the muscle from balling up while you sleep.

First, before bed, stretch your calf against the wall for thirty seconds on each side, front knee bent, back heel down. Second, once in bed, pull your toes up toward your shin ten times on each foot. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, leave the blanket loose around your feet instead of tucked in tight, so your foot is not pointed all night. Nothing in your calf is broken. It just spent the day being told to stay short, and at night it finally reacted.
