Why your heel only hurts for the first few steps

You were never told why your heel stings on your very first steps in the morning. Stay with me, this is quick.

Overnight your foot hangs pointed down, and your calf and the sole of your foot spend the night held short. Picture yourself taking a stick of gum straight from the fridge and biting into it: hard and stinging on the first chew, soft after a few more. Your first step in the morning is that first chew, pulling the cold, short tissue long in one go, which is why it stings at the start and eases after a few steps.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether it is really your foot staying folded overnight.

You know the pattern. Stepping out of bed and going straight for your heel before your toes touch the floor. The sting fades by the third or fourth step, almost like it was never there. Standing barefoot on cold tile feels sharper than on carpet. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it, and it is the one that keeps the tissue from going cold and short overnight.

First, while still in bed, pull your foot toward you ten times before you stand up. Second, sit on the edge of the bed and stretch your calf for thirty seconds on each side before your feet touch the floor. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, in the evening, roll the sole of your foot over a bottle for sixty seconds, so it does not start the night already short. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which side is really staying folded overnight.

Nothing in your heel is broken. It was just cold and short for a few hours, and your first step had to warm it back up.
