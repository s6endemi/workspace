Your hip forgets how long it is supposed to be

Does your hip lock up the moment you get out of the car? Save this video, in a moment I show you the three things that loosen it again. Stay with me, this is quick.

After an hour bent in the seat, your hip flexor forgets how long it is supposed to be. It stays short, the way a pocketknife blade stays folded even after you lift it from your pocket. When you plant your feet and try to stand tall, the muscle will not give the length back, so your lower back arches hard to finish the job the hip refuses to do.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether it is really your hip that stayed folded.

You know the signs. The first steps after climbing out feel shorter on one side. Standing fully upright takes a beat too long. Getting up from a low chair right afterwards feels the same, stiff and half open. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it before they even leave the car, and it is the one that stops the hinge from locking.

First, before you get out, tilt your pelvis forward for five seconds while still seated, giving the hip flexor one last stretch. Second, once standing, take ten steps with a deliberately long stride, letting the back leg fully open behind you. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, in the evening, hold a half kneeling lunge for thirty seconds on each side, so the hip flexor remembers its full length. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see which hip actually stayed folded.

Nothing in your hip is broken. It just spent an hour being told to stay short, and it believed you.
