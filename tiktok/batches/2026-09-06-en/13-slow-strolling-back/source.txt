Why Slow Strolling Wrecks Your Back More Than Hiking

Does twenty minutes of window shopping leave your back more sore than an entire hike? Nobody ever explained why. Stay with me, this is quick.

Real walking swings the hip back with every stride, so your lower back can ride along loosely. Slow strolling never lets the hip finish that swing, the step stays half, and the lower back locks upright to carry your torso alone. Picture yourself driving through stop and go traffic: you crawl, brake, crawl again, and burn more fuel than on the open highway even though you are going slower.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether your hip is actually finishing that stride.

You know the feeling. A slow walk through a museum leaves your back tighter than the stairs to the garage. Standing at a shop window already means shifting your weight foot to foot. Walking beside someone slower feels more tiring than walking at your own pace. And especially the third fix below, almost nobody does it before an evening of slow walking, and it is the one that gives the hip its stride back.

First, every few minutes, take twenty brisk steps with a long stride, letting the back leg stretch fully behind you. Second, when you pause at a shop window, plant one foot forward and shift your weight between your legs, ten times. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, in the evening, hold a half kneeling lunge for thirty seconds each side, so the front of the hip remembers how to open. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see whether your hip is really finishing the stride.

Nothing in your back is broken. It just spent twenty minutes holding you up because your hip never got to help.
