Back pain when you stand for long is not what they keep telling you

Your back hurts when you stand for a long time? It is not what you think, and not what you keep being told.

Everyone tells you: weak back, train your core. But watch yourself in a queue. Knees locked straight, hips pushed forward, ribcage leaning back. You are standing like a tent pole that is jammed into the ground. Nothing moves, so the only thing holding you up is the small muscles of your lower back, and they are the ones that burn after ten minutes.

Previa Health shows you in a short test whether you stand on your joints or on your muscles.

You know the signs. At a concert your back hurts, on a walk it does not. You shift from one leg to the other without noticing. You lean on every counter you can find. And especially number three, the fix nobody does, is the one that makes standing quiet again.

First, unlock the knees. Bend them a few millimetres so they are soft, not straight. Feel how the weight moves into your thighs. Second, the ribcage down. Breathe out fully and let the ribs sink toward the hips, hold that for five breaths. If you want more small tricks like this, feel free to follow Previa Health. Third, shift your weight. Every two minutes move it from heel to toe and from one foot to the other, so no muscle has to hold still for long.

With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app, and see where your body freezes when you stand.

Your back is not weak. It has been holding still for you, because everything else was locked.
