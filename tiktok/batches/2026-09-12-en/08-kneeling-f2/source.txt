What An Hour On Your Knees Actually Does To Your Legs

If you are building furniture this weekend or spending an hour weeding the garden, stay with me for a second. Three things, and they change how you feel when you get back up.

Here's the mechanic: kneeling is fine, holding one single kneeling position for an hour is not. Your whole weight parks on one small spot, the leg underneath stops moving completely, and nothing in that hour ever asks it for a change. Picture yourself kneeling on the kitchen floor, half under the sink, tightening a pipe with both hands: your weight sits on one knee on the hard tiles, and the longer you are under there the less you move, until you back out and the first steps to the door are careful little ones.

First thing: kneel on one knee with the other foot flat on the floor in front of you, and swap sides every few minutes, so that no side gets the whole hour. Second thing: every fifteen minutes stand up all the way, straighten out and take five steps, even if the job is not finished. If you want more of this, feel free to follow Previa Health.

And the third one, almost nobody does this: when you get up, put one foot flat in front of you and stand up over that foot instead of pushing off your thighs with both hands. Then walk five steps before you pick anything up.

Nothing about your legs is worn out here. They simply spent an hour in one position with nothing to do. With Previa Health you can see how this looks in your own body in about two minutes on your phone, free, no app.
