Why Kids Almost Never Complain About Back Pain

Kids almost never complain about back pain, and the reason isn't their age.

It's because a kid never really stops moving. They squat, they climb, they lie flat, they hang upside down, and nothing stays in one spot for long. Picture yourself at a playground for an afternoon: the kids have squatted down by the sandbox, hung from the monkey bars, rolled around in the grass and climbed the slide backwards, while you sat on the same bench in the same position the entire time.

That difference shows up the moment you both get up. The kid pops straight off the ground like nothing happened. You peel yourself off that bench stiffer than you sat down, even though you did far less. And here's the part that surprises people every time: your back usually isn't complaining because you carried something heavy, it's complaining because you didn't change position enough. People who shift between a handful of different positions through the day tend to feel better than people who hold the same one or two positions for a long stretch, no matter how comfortable that stretch felt at the time.

So here's what actually helps. Somewhere in your day, steal back a few positions adults quietly stop using once they grow up. Drop into a full squat like you're tying a shoe.
