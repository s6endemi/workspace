Why You Turn Over Thirty Times Every Single Night

You change position around thirty times a night, and you remember not a single one of them. That is not restlessness. That is the whole point.

No position survives a whole night. After twenty or thirty minutes in one shape, your body quietly arranges the next one, long before anything starts to ache, and it does that all night without ever waking you up for it. Picture yourself asleep on a friend's narrow couch, one arm's width across: every time you try to turn you nearly roll off, so you stay exactly where you are until morning, and you get up sorer than after a whole day of walking.

And here's the part almost nobody expects: the people who wake up stiff are often the ones who moved least, not the ones who tossed and turned. It is why a night in a packed tent or a cramped train seat costs you more than a short night at home ever does. It is why a bed you can turn in matters more than the mattress inside it.

None of this means a restless night is a bad night, it means turning over is part of sleeping, not a sign that something went wrong. So here's the payoff: give yourself room to turn, a blanket that is not tucked in tight and space on both sides.
