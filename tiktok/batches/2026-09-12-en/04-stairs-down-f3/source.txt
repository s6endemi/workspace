Downstairs Walking Trains A Skill Nobody Practices

Walking down the stairs works your legs harder than walking up them. That is not a sign of weakness, it is a sign of a skill almost nobody trains.

Going up, your legs push you forward, and the movement is simple, familiar work. Going down, your legs have to catch your whole weight on every single step, and that braking is a completely different job most days never ask for. Picture yourself carrying a full pot of soup down a narrow staircase: every step you have to catch the weight softly so nothing spills, and by the time you reach the bottom your legs are shaking, even though you never lifted a thing.

That is why a downhill hike leaves you far sorer the next day than the climb up ever did, while your lungs barely noticed a difference either way. It is why stepping down off a bus or off a curb is the exact moment your leg has to catch your entire body in an instant. And here is the part almost nobody thinks about: that same braking is usually the first thing to feel unsteady later on, long before climbing stairs ever gets hard.

So once a day, walk down five extra stairs on purpose, slow, three seconds on every step. In the evening, ten slow squats train the same skill: three seconds going down, then back up at a normal pace. If you want more of this, feel free to follow Previa Health.
