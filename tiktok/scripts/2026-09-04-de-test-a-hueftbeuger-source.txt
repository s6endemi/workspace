Your back hurts after sitting because of a muscle nobody told you about

Your back hurts after sitting? It is not your back. And nobody has ever told you this. You did not know that? Then listen very closely, because this changes what you do about it.

The culprit sits at the front of your hips. Two thick muscles, the hip flexors, run from your spine over the front of your pelvis to your thighs. Every hour in a chair keeps them short. Then you stand up, they stay short, and they pull your pelvis forward like a tent rope that is tied too tight. The pole has to lean. That pole is your lower back.

So the ache you feel after the car, after the office, after the sofa is your back doing extra work for a muscle that stopped letting go. And you probably know the signs already. Your lower back feels stiff when you first stand up. You arch your back when you reach up high. Standing still for ten minutes feels worse than walking. And especially number three coming up, almost nobody connects it: one side of your back always feels tighter than the other, because one hip is usually shorter.

Here is how you give the muscle its length back. First, the couch stretch. Kneel with one knee against the sofa, shin up the backrest, and stand tall for thirty seconds each side. No pushing, just breathing. Second, slow glute bridges. Lie on your back and lift your hips ten times, pausing two seconds at the top, so the back of your body takes over the job.
