If your eyes burn by 3pm, move before you rub them

Your eyes start burning at 3pm. You squeeze them shut, rub them, maybe lean closer to the screen because the words suddenly feel harder to hold. The strange part is that your eyes may not be the only thing that has been working without a break.

During a focused screen block, your gaze stays on one small rectangle. Your head often becomes still. Your upper back barely changes position. You can be mentally busy for four hours while physically holding almost the same pose. That fixed near focus and low movement can make an ordinary workday feel surprisingly intense by the afternoon.

With PREVIA, you can test this pattern yourself: take a quick look at how much your head, upper back, and shoulders stay locked during a screen task. You are not looking for a diagnosis. You are looking for a pattern you can notice before the day catches up with you.

Try this before reaching for another coffee. Stand up. Pick a point far across the room and let your eyes rest there for twenty seconds. While you look, slowly turn your head left and right twice, then roll your shoulders back without forcing them down. Go back to work with the screen a little farther away if you can.

The point is not to turn a workday into a mobility routine. It is to give your gaze and posture a new job for half a minute. If the burning keeps showing up, the useful question is not whether you can tolerate it longer. It is how many hours passed without your body changing position.

Do this once before your usual afternoon crash. You may notice the screen feels less like a magnet when your whole upper body gets permission to move.
