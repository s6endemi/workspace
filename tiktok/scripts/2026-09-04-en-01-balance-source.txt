If balancing on one leg while getting dressed feels shaky, save this.

The problem may not be that you suddenly lost your balance. It may be a skill your day has stopped asking for.

Balance is not one muscle. It is a quiet conversation between the sole of your foot, your ankle, your hip, and your eyes. On an uneven path, your ankle makes tiny corrections, your hip shifts over the standing leg, and your foot tells your brain where the ground is. You barely notice any of it.

Most days now look very different. Chair. Car. Smooth floor. Firm shoes. Then one rushed moment on one leg while pulling on a sock, stepping off a curb, or reaching into the back seat. It is like expecting a camera tripod to stay steady after one leg has been folded away all day.

A free PREVIA movement scan can make that handoff visible. It looks at how your ankles, hips, and trunk share load in simple movements, so you can see where your body starts borrowing stability instead of guessing from one wobbly moment.

Try this at your kitchen counter tonight. Keep one hand lightly on the counter, stand on one foot for twenty seconds, then switch. Keep the standing knee soft and let the arch of the foot stay relaxed instead of clawing the floor. Next, stand barefoot with both feet under your hips and slowly shift your weight from one foot to the other for ten passes. Feel the whole foot meet the floor. Finish by stepping slowly onto the first stair, pausing on one leg before you bring the other foot up. Do five each side while holding the railing.

This is not about performing a perfect balance test. It is about giving your body small chances to use a skill modern life keeps removing. Save this for the next time getting dressed on one leg feels harder than it should.
