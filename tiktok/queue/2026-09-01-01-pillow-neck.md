The pillow industry keeps selling you a neck problem you built before bed

A new pillow. A cooling pillow. A contour pillow with a price tag that hurts more than your neck. You keep being told the answer starts at 11pm.

But your neck has already been working overtime since breakfast.

On the train, at your laptop, over dinner, your chin drifts forward and your upper back barely moves. The small muscles at the base of your skull pull all day just to keep your eyes level. By bedtime they feel like tent ropes holding up a leaning pole. Then you lie down and expect a better pillow to undo ten hours of that job.

A pillow can make a position more comfortable. It cannot give a stiff upper back its movement back. That is the part the pillow aisle never has to talk about.

With PREVIA, you can test this yourself in a few minutes: the movement scan can show whether your head, shoulders, and upper back are compensating before you buy another sleep product.

Try this tonight instead. Stand against a wall and make eight slow arm slides without letting your ribs pop forward. Then lie on the floor with a folded towel under your upper back and take five calm breaths into the sides of your ribs. Finish with 30 seconds of gentle chin nods, not a hard stretch.

The point is not to force your neck straight. It is to stop asking your neck to do your upper back's job.

A 90-euro pillow may feel nice. Two quiet minutes before bed may tell you why the old one never changed much.
