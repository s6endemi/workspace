Why your back feels worse on a quiet weekend

If your back feels worse on Sunday evening than it did on a busy Wednesday, the problem may be how little your body changed position all day.

A quiet weekend can look restful from the outside. Coffee on the sofa. A long movie. A few hours with your laptop. Then your lower back starts asking for attention every time you stand up.

Your back is not built to hold one position for hours, even a comfortable one. During a normal weekday, you get small doses of movement without noticing: stairs, errands, walking to get lunch, standing while you talk. On a slow day, those little changes disappear. Your hips stay bent. Your rib cage barely rotates. The muscles around your trunk keep doing the same low-level job for too long.

That is why stretching hard at 8pm can feel disappointing. You are trying to pull length into a body that has mostly been still. It often responds better to a little variety first.

Try this the next time a quiet day leaves you stiff. Stand up and do five slow sit-to-stands. Take a relaxed two-minute walk around your home, letting your arms swing. Then spend 30 seconds with your hands on a counter and gently shift your hips back and forth. Nothing dramatic. You are reminding your body that it has more than one setting.

If your back pain is new, severe, follows an injury, or comes with numbness or weakness, get medical advice rather than treating a video as an answer.
