# Your toes may be why one leg does all the work

## Script
If one leg always feels stronger on stairs, look at your toes before you blame the stronger leg.

Watch someone climb a flight of stairs when they are tired. One foot lands flat. The other pushes off cleanly through the big toe. The hip on that side does more work, the knee stays calmer, and the whole step looks easier. We barely notice this because feet disappear inside shoes all day.

Your big toe is supposed to bend as you push the ground away. That small movement helps your foot become a stable lever. When it does not happen comfortably, your body can take a detour. You might turn the foot outward to avoid the bend. You might roll toward the outside edge. Your calf may stay switched on, and the other leg quietly becomes the reliable one on stairs, lunges, or a brisk walk.

This does not mean a stiff toe explains every knee or hip issue. It means the foot is part of the chain, and it is one of the easiest places to stop checking.

Take off your shoes. Sit down and gently lift just the big toe ten times while the other toes stay relaxed. Then place the foot on the floor and slowly shift weight forward until the big toe bends, without forcing it. Finish with eight easy step-ups per side. Make the push-off quiet and controlled, as if you are relearning where the floor is.

Feet do not need punishment. They need a little attention and room to do their job.
