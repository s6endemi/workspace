## Skript (EN, NotebookLM-Quelle)

Why a low chair makes your legs shake when a normal chair does not

You drop into a deep sofa after work. Ten minutes later you stand up and your thighs shake like you just finished a hard workout. A normal chair did not do that to you. The low seat did.

That is not a verdict on your body. It is a clue about the movement the sofa quietly demands. From a low seat, your hips begin deeper, your knees travel farther forward, and your feet need to stay connected to the floor while your torso shifts forward. If one piece arrives late, your thighs often do the loudest emergency work.

This is why the moment can feel strange after a day of sitting. Your body has spent hours in one position, then the sofa asks for a coordinated stand-up from a deeper angle. You push with the front of your thighs, hold your breath for a second, and rise in two moves instead of one.

With PREVIA, you can use a phone-based movement check to see where your squat and leg-raise patterns start compensating. It gives you something more useful than guessing that your legs are simply weak.

Try a calm version of the movement. Sit on a firm chair, not the deep sofa. Put both feet flat and slightly in front of your knees. Lean your chest forward until your nose is over your toes, then press the floor away and stand without throwing your arms backward. Sit down slowly. Do five repetitions. If it feels easy, pause for two seconds just above the seat on the way down.

The goal is not to chase a burn. It is to let your feet, hips, and thighs share the job again. Notice whether the next stand-up looks and feels less dramatic.
