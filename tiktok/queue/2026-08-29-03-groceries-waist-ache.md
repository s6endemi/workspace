## Script

Why carrying one grocery bag can make the opposite side of your waist ache

The bag is in your right hand. The ache appears on the left side of your waist. That feels backwards, until you see what your body is trying to do.

A heavy bag pulls one side of your pelvis down. To keep your head level and your coffee from spilling, the muscles on the other side of your trunk tighten to hold you upright. For a short walk, that is a perfectly normal solution. The problem is repetition. Same hand, same route, same side doing the stabilising, day after day.

That is why the ache can show up after you have put the bag down. You feel it reaching into the washing machine, standing at the counter, or rolling over in bed. It is not always the side that carried the weight. It may be the side that quietly worked to stop you leaning.

Use a simple check the next time you walk home. Switch the bag to the other hand halfway through, even if the new side feels clumsy. When you get home, stand with both feet under your hips and slowly shift your weight right, then left, for 20 seconds. Follow with four relaxed side reaches per side. Keep the movement small enough that your ribs stay easy.

You are not trying to build a stronger waist in your hallway. You are giving both sides a turn at the job.

## Caption

Die Einkaufstasche hängt rechts, aber links zieht die Taille. Das ist nicht so widersprüchlich, wie es klingt. Damit Kopf und Oberkörper gerade bleiben, arbeitet häufig die Gegenseite gegen den Zug der Tasche. Wechsel die Hand unterwegs, verlagere zu Hause kurz dein Gewicht nach beiden Seiten und mach ein paar kleine Seitneigungen. Das reicht oft als freundlicher Ausgleich nach dem Heimweg. Speichere dir das für den nächsten Einkauf.
