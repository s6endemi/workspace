# Why your shoulder pinches when you reach for the seat belt

- Format: symptom
- Experiment: none

## Script

Why your shoulder pinches when you reach for the seat belt

That quick reach for the seat belt should be nothing. Yet sometimes your shoulder catches halfway back, then feels fine once you have forced the belt across your chest.

The shoulder may not be the whole story. In the car, your upper back is pressed into the seat, your ribs hardly rotate, and one arm has been resting forward on the wheel. When you reach back and up for the belt, the shoulder blade needs to slide and tip while the arm rotates. If the upper back has been held still for an hour, the front of the shoulder can end up doing more of the work than it likes.

You might notice it in other small moments. Reaching for a jacket sleeve feels pinchy. Washing your hair makes one side feel crowded. You can lift your arm in front of you, but the diagonal reach behind you is the one that complains.

Try a small reset before you leave the car. Sit or stand tall and take three slow breaths into the sides of your ribs. Then let one arm make five slow circles, as if you are drawing a dinner plate on the wall. Keep the shoulder relaxed rather than yanking it back. Finally, place your palm on a door frame at shoulder height and turn your chest away a few centimeters for twenty seconds. Stop if it produces pain rather than a mild stretch.

This does not diagnose a shoulder problem, and a persistent or painful catch deserves proper assessment. It can simply be a useful clue that a stiff upper back has changed how the shoulder has to move.

## Redakteur-Prompt

Create a German explanatory video, 60 to 90 seconds maximum, never longer. Use curious, visual Kurzgesagt-style storytelling. Open with the tiny but instantly recognizable car moment: the seat belt reach catches in one shoulder. Reveal that this reach asks for arm rotation plus a moving shoulder blade and an upper back that can rotate, while a long drive holds the upper back and ribs still. Make the cascade concrete: jacket sleeves, washing hair, diagonal reach behind the body, and the front of one shoulder feeling crowded. Show three low intensity resets with exact counts and timing: three rib breaths, five easy arm circles, then a 20-second gentle doorway turn. Say these can help and must not be forced. Include the short safety note that persistent or painful catching needs assessment. End with one casual PREVIA sentence, no pitch. Every sentence must earn its place.
