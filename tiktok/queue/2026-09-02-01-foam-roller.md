Your foam roller is not fixing the part that keeps locking up

The foam roller industry has made you believe every tight spot needs more pressure.

Roll your calves. Dig into your upper back. Find the painful spot and stay on it until it hurts enough to feel productive. Then, an hour later, that same place feels locked again.

Here is the awkward part they do not put on the box. A roller can make a sore area feel different for a while. It cannot change the position your body returns to for the next eight hours.

If your upper back barely rotates, your ribs stay quiet, and your shoulders live in front of you at the laptop, one small patch of muscle often has to keep doing extra work. Pressing harder on that patch is like repeatedly silencing a smoke alarm while the toast is still burning.

With PREVIA, you can test this yourself in a few minutes. The movement scan can show where your upper body is compensating instead of guessing which painful spot to attack.

Try a different reset tonight. Put one hand on a doorframe, take a small step through, and turn your chest away slowly for 30 seconds per side. Then sit tall on a chair and make six slow rotations, following your hand with your eyes. Finish with five breaths into the side ribs, not just the top of your chest.

None of that is flashy. It gives your upper body more places to move, so one spot does not have to carry the whole shift.

A foam roller can still feel good. It is just a very expensive answer if the same desk position is waiting for you five minutes later.
