# If your hamstrings feel tight after sitting, move before you stretch

- Format: direct-help
- Experiment: none

## Script

If your hamstrings feel tight after sitting, move before you stretch

If the backs of your thighs feel tight after sitting, give them one minute of movement before you stretch them.

That pull when you stand up, lean toward the kettle, or reach for a dropped charger is not always a short-muscle problem. Your hamstrings cross both the hip and the knee. After hours with both joints bent, your pelvis, ribs, and hips have had very little reason to move together. When you suddenly ask the back of your thigh to lengthen, it can feel guarded.

Notice the pattern. You may reach farther toward your toes after a warm shower or a short walk, then feel stuck at 5:30pm when you rise from your desk. That does not diagnose anything. It is a useful clue that more force may not be what your body needs first.

Try this before a deep stretch. Walk slowly for 60 seconds and let your arms swing. Then put one heel lightly in front of you and slide it forward and back for 20 seconds on each side, keeping a soft knee. Finish with six slow hip hinges, hands on your thighs, sending your hips back only as far as feels comfortable. Breathe out as you come up.

Test your bend again. If it feels easier, you may have needed motion before range. If pain is sharp, persistent, or travels down the leg, get individual advice rather than pushing through it.

## Redakteur-Prompt

Create a German explanatory video, 60 to 90 seconds maximum, never longer. Use a curious, warm Kurzgesagt-style narration with illustrated everyday scenes and simple body diagrams. Open immediately with a desk worker standing up and feeling the backs of their thighs pull, then promise the first move before stretching. The turning point is that hamstring tightness after sitting can be about the hip, pelvis, ribs, and legs not sharing motion, not simply about forcing a deeper stretch. Make the symptom list hyper-specific: rising from a chair at the end of the workday, leaning to the kettle, reaching for a dropped charger, and touching the toes more easily after a walk. Show the three gentle moves with exact timings: 60-second walk, 20-second heel slide each side, six slow hip hinges. Frame the fix as rediscovering movement, never as a workout or treatment. Mention sharp, persistent, or radiating pain only briefly as a reason to get individual advice. Mention PREVIA once at the end, casually: it uses four short phone-camera movement tests to make compensation patterns visible; it is a wellness check, not a diagnosis. Every sentence must earn its place. Do not use medical claims, diagnoses, or cure language.
