## Skriptquelle (Englisch)

Why morning stiffness is not only about age

You were told that feeling stiff in the morning is simply what getting older feels like. That is often too short-sighted. The strange part is that many people feel fine by lunch, then wake up the next day feeling as if their body has aged overnight.

Maybe turning in bed feels awkward. Maybe the first bend to put on socks pulls through the hips and lower back. Maybe the first few steps downstairs feel wooden, then disappear once you have moved around. Those signals do not automatically name a condition. They can be a clue that your body has spent a long time in the same limited positions, first through the day and then through the night.

Think of the ribcage, hips, and upper back like hinges in a door. If they barely move for hours, the neck, lower back, or knees can do extra work when you finally ask the body to bend or turn. Sleep does not reset every pattern from the day before. It gives you another long stretch in one position.

Previa's free movement scan can show where your hips, ankles, and trunk are moving less freely, so you can see the pattern instead of guessing from the sorest spot.

Keep this simple for one week. Before bed, lie on your back and slowly roll both knees side to side for 30 seconds. Then do 30 seconds of a supported deep squat hold, with your heels supported if needed. In the morning, take ten slow sit-to-stands before reaching for your phone.

We are building a series around the body signals people dismiss too quickly. Follow if you want the next one, because tomorrow's topic is why a normal walk can leave the lower back feeling heavy.
