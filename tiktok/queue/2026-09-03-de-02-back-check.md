Three places to check when low back tightness keeps returning

A tight low back after driving, a pull when you bend to load the dishwasher, or that one side that always feels harder to turn is often treated like a back-only problem.

But the low back sits between two busy neighbors. Above it, your ribcage needs to rotate as you reach and walk. Below it, your hips and ankles need enough movement to let you sit, stand, and step without borrowing motion from the same few spinal segments.

When those areas stop contributing, the low back can become the place that keeps making small adjustments. It is like one person carrying a sofa because everyone else has let go. The person is not the problem. They are doing too much of the carrying.

That is why stretching the sore spot can feel good for an hour but not change the pattern. A free PREVIA movement scan can show how your trunk, hips, knees, and ankles work together, so the starting point is based on your movement rather than the loudest symptom.

Here are three calm checks to save. Sit tall with your arms crossed and rotate gently five times each way. Notice whether your ribs can turn without your hips following immediately. Then try a half-kneeling hip glide for thirty seconds per side, staying upright and easy. Finally, face a wall and bring each knee toward it ten times while the heel stays down. That gives you a simple look at ankle travel.

Keep every test pain-free and small. You are not trying to force range. You are finding which neighbor has stopped helping. We are building a series around these everyday movement clues, so follow if you want the next one before your back has to do all the work again.
