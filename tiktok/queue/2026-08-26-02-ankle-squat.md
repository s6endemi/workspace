# The reason your heels leave the floor

## Script
The reason your heels leave the floor is usually not that you are bad at squatting.

You drop down, your hips feel fine, then your heels start lifting. Your knees drift forward, your chest folds over your thighs, and suddenly a bodyweight squat feels like a balance trick.

Most people blame their hips. But the bottleneck is often much lower: your ankle needs to travel forward over your foot while your heel stays planted. If that motion is limited, your body still wants to get down. It just borrows movement somewhere else. Your feet turn out more. Your arches may collapse inward. Your knees may slide in. Your low back rounds early because it is looking for range that the ankle did not give it.

This is why tight calves are not always a calf problem. They can be the alarm bell for a movement you stopped using.

Try this for a week. Stand facing a wall with one foot a hand-width away. Keep that heel down and let the knee gently travel toward the wall for 45 seconds on each side. Then sit into a supported deep squat while holding a doorframe or counter. Stay there for 30 seconds and breathe slowly. Finish with ten slow heel raises, pressing through the big toe.

You are not forcing flexibility. You are reminding your ankle that it is allowed to move forward again.
