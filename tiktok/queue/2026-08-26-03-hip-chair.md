# Why standing up hurts more than sitting down

## Script
The first two seconds after you stand up can tell you more than the whole hour you spent sitting.

You have been at your desk, you stand, and there is that familiar pull across the front of the hip or a brief complaint from the low back. Then you walk three steps and it mostly disappears. That pattern feels random. It is usually your body switching jobs too fast.

While you sit, your hips stay bent and your glutes are relatively quiet. When you stand, your hip needs to open while the glutes help extend it. If that handover is clumsy, your lower back often tries to finish the movement first. You may feel yourself lean back before you are fully upright. Your ribs flare. One foot takes more weight. You rub the front of one hip without even noticing.

The answer is not to panic about sitting, or to stretch aggressively between meetings. Give the transition a better option.

Before your next meeting, stand with one hand on your desk. Put one foot behind you and gently squeeze that glute for five seconds. Repeat five times per side. Then do six slow sit-to-stands from your chair. Let your ribs stay stacked over your pelvis and let both feet press evenly into the floor. Finish by taking ten relaxed walking steps before you hurry anywhere.

The goal is to make standing feel ordinary again, rather than a small negotiation with your back.
