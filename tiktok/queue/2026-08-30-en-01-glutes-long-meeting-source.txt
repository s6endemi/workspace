Why your glutes feel absent after a long meeting

Your glutes can feel like they have clocked out after a long meeting. You stand up, the first few steps feel wooden, and your lower back or hamstrings seem eager to do all the work.

That does not mean your glutes have literally switched off. A chair keeps your hips bent and gives the biggest hip muscles very little reason to extend your leg, steady your pelvis, or help you push into the floor. After an hour of almost no change, standing asks for all of those jobs at once. Your body can take a few steps to spread the work around again.

You may notice it when you stand from a low sofa, climb the first flight of stairs, pick up a bag, or try to walk quickly to your next call. If you lean into one hip while listening, one side may feel slower than the other. That is common, but it is not a diagnosis.

Try a small reset before you sit through the next block. Stand with both feet under your hips and gently squeeze your glutes for five slow breaths. Keep your ribs stacked over your pelvis instead of arching your back. Then do five controlled sit-to-stands from the chair, pressing through your whole foot as you rise. Finish with a 30-second walk, letting your arms swing naturally.

These are not magic fixes. They simply give your hips more than one job after they have been parked for a while. If you have persistent numbness, clear weakness, severe pain, or symptoms after a fall, get medical advice rather than trying to solve it with a social-media routine.
