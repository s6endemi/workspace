Why You Keep Wanting To Crack Your Back At Your Desk

If you keep twisting in your chair trying to crack your back, save this video. In a moment I show you the three places to move before your next desk block. You did not know that? Then listen very closely.

That urge often shows up after your ribs and hips have spent hours facing one direction while your eyes stay glued to one rectangle. Your back is not asking for a dramatic twist. It is asking for the small turns it missed all day.

Imagine a door with three rusty hinges. The handle still turns, so you pull harder and harder, but the door only opens when each hinge gets some movement. Your neck, ribcage and hips can work like that after a long desk spell. When one stays stuck, another part twists too far trying to make up the difference.

Previa Health can help you check where that movement is missing in a short phone self-test.

You notice it in ordinary moments: you twist to grab your coffee and feel one side pull first. You turn to reverse the car and move your whole body before your head catches up. You roll over in bed and your mid-back feels like it needs a running start. Especially number three is why the chair twist keeps coming back.

First, sit tall at the front edge of your chair, put one hand on the opposite knee, and turn your chest slowly five times each way. Second, stand beside the chair, put both hands on your ribs, and breathe into the side that barely moves for five slow breaths.

Follow Previa Health so you do not miss the next body signal.

Third, step one foot behind you at the desk, bend the front knee slightly, and rotate your chest toward that knee five times per side. With Previa Health you can test this yourself on your phone in about two minutes, free, no app.

The next time your chair twist calls your name, give all three hinges a turn first.
