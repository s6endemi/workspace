## Skriptquelle (Englisch)

Why more cushioning does not solve tired feet

If your feet burn after standing, the first thing you are offered is usually more cushioning. Softer insoles. Bigger shoes. Another layer between you and the ground. But that can miss the part of the story happening inside your shoe.

Your foot is not one solid block. It has small muscles, joints, and a big toe that all need to share load when you walk and stand. When a narrow toe box keeps the toes pressed together, or when the foot has spent years doing very little variety, the big toe may not contribute much as you push off. Other areas can take over. That is when a long queue, a city walk, or an afternoon on your feet can feel far bigger than it should.

This is also why some people buy softer and softer shoes yet still feel tired at the end of the day. The cushion changes the surface. It does not show how the ankle, hip, and foot are sharing the job.

Previa's free movement scan can show where your lower-body movement starts compensating, including the ankle and hip patterns that travel down to the foot. It takes a few minutes with your phone.

Try this tonight. Stand barefoot near a wall. Keep the heel down and gently move one knee toward the wall ten times without the arch collapsing. Then spread your toes, let the big toe stay on the floor, and hold that position for 20 seconds. Finally, take a short walk and notice whether you can push the ground behind you through the whole foot instead of rolling to the outside.

You do not need to throw away your shoes. You may just need your feet to take part in the job again.
