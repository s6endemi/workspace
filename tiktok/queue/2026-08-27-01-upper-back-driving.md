## Skript (EN, NotebookLM-Quelle)

Why your upper back locks up after driving

That stiff spot between your shoulder blades after a car ride is not always asking for a harder stretch.

You step out of the car, twist to grab your bag, and your upper back feels like it has been glued to the seat. You roll your shoulders, pull your chest up, and it barely changes. Then you walk for a few minutes and the whole area starts to feel more normal. That is a very specific clue.

During a drive, your upper back is doing a quiet job for a long time. Your hands stay forward, your ribs barely rotate, and one arm may keep making tiny steering corrections. The middle of your spine is built to share rotation with your ribs and shoulders. When it gets almost none for an hour, the next turn can feel surprisingly expensive. Your neck often joins in, because it is trying to look over a shoulder while the rest of the system stays still.

Try a small reset at your next stop. Sit tall and place both hands across your chest. Slowly turn to the right for five relaxed breaths, then to the left for five. Stand beside the car, rest one hand on the roof, and let your chest gently rotate away from that hand for 20 seconds per side. Finish by walking for one minute with your arms swinging normally.

You are not trying to force your back open. You are giving your ribs, shoulders, and upper back the movement they missed while driving.
