# The perfect office chair is a very expensive distraction

**Format:** A-leak

## Script source

The perfect office chair is a very expensive distraction. Lumbar support. Adjustable arms. Breathable mesh. A headrest that costs extra. The furniture industry keeps selling upgrades because it is easier to sell you a better chair than admit what no chair can do: make eight still hours feel natural.

Here is what nobody connects. By late afternoon, the issue is often not that your chair failed. Your hips have stayed bent, your upper back has barely moved, and your eyes have kept your head drifting toward the screen. A premium chair can make that position more comfortable. It cannot turn it into movement.

That is why the signs can feel so random. Your lower back feels tired when you stand up. Your shoulders creep toward your ears during a normal email. You shift, cross one leg, then the other, and nothing feels right. The chair is not necessarily bad. Your body is asking for a change it cannot get from another adjustment knob.

PREVIA's free movement scan can show where your body is compensating before you blame the next chair. It takes five minutes on your phone.

Try the thing no brochure sells. Every 45 minutes, stand up and walk for one minute. Then do five slow sit-to-stands without dropping back into the seat. Finish with five easy upper-back turns each way, keeping your hips facing forward. You are not fixing posture. You are giving the positions you have avoided all day a chance to come back online.

A better chair can be nice. But a chair cannot do your moving for you. Before buying another accessory, interrupt the stillness that made you want it.

## Caption

That expensive chair may feel better, but it cannot replace the movement your body misses during a long screen day. If you keep shifting, your shoulders keep creeping up, or standing feels worse than sitting, the answer may be fewer adjustments and more position changes. Save this for your next workday.
