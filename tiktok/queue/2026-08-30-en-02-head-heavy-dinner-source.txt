Why your head feels heavier by dinner

By dinner, your head can feel strangely heavy even though you have been sitting all day. Your neck feels tired, your shoulders creep upward, and turning to check traffic on the way home suddenly feels less smooth.

Your head has not gained weight. The work around it has just become very repetitive. During a screen day, your eyes stay on one small area, your arms reach toward a keyboard, and your upper back barely changes shape. The muscles that help hold your head and shoulder blades in place can spend hours making tiny adjustments. A still posture is not automatically harmful, but it can become tiring when it is the only posture you use.

In more than 250 PREVIA movement assessments, 64 percent showed forward head posture. That does not explain every sore neck, and it does not tell you what is wrong with yours. It is a useful reminder that the view from your screen can quietly change how the rest of your upper body organizes itself.

Use a reset that feels almost too simple. First, stand up and let both arms hang for ten seconds while you breathe normally. Then place your forearms on a wall at shoulder height and slowly slide them up and down five times, only as far as feels comfortable. Finish with a one-minute walk where you look ahead, then gently left and right instead of staring at the ground.

You are not trying to force perfect posture. You are giving your neck, ribs, shoulder blades, and eyes a few new positions before the day ends.
