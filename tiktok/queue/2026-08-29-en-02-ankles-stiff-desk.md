## Script

Why your ankles feel stiff when you stand up after your desk

You stand up after a few hours at your desk and your ankles feel like they did not get the memo. The first steps can feel wooden. Your feet may land flatter than usual, and walking to the kitchen takes a few seconds to feel normal again.

Your ankles are small joints with a busy job. Every step asks them to bend forward, let your heel come down, shift weight over the foot, and push you forward again. During a long seated stretch, they barely travel through that range. Your calf muscles and the tissues around the ankle are not necessarily tight in some permanent way. They have simply been quiet in one position while the rest of your day kept moving on a screen.

That is why the stiffness often appears in ordinary moments: the first stair after a meeting, reaching for the printer, or getting up to answer the door. It can be more obvious if your chair keeps your feet tucked underneath you or if one foot always hooks around the other.

Before you stand, make ten slow ankle circles each way. Then keep your heels on the floor and gently lift your toes ten times. When you get up, take ten easy steps and let your heel touch down before you hurry. If it feels comfortable, finish with five slow heel raises while holding a desk or wall for balance.

These are not magic drills. They are a reminder that your ankles are built for a moving day, not just the walk from chair to coffee machine. Sudden swelling, warmth, severe pain, or trouble bearing weight should be checked by a clinician.
