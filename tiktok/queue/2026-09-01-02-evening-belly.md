Your stomach can feel swollen by evening even when food was not the whole story

By seven o'clock your waistband feels tighter, you sit down after work, and your stomach feels like it is pushing outward. You did not suddenly gain weight between lunch and dinner. You may not even have eaten much.

One overlooked piece is the way your trunk has been held all day.

When you spend hours folded over a keyboard, in a car, or on a sofa, your lower ribs move less and your hip flexors stay short. Your diaphragm still has to work under that rib cage. Your abdominal wall often answers by bracing, as if it has been asked to hold a belt tight for ten hours. It can leave your middle feeling tense and crowded by the evening.

That does not make every tight stomach a posture story. It does explain why some people feel different after they stand, walk, and let their ribs move again.

With PREVIA, you can test the movement side early: the scan shows where your rib cage, hips, and trunk are moving around each other instead of together.

Here is the small reset most people never try. Put both hands around your lower ribs and take five slow breaths outward into your hands. Then do a 45-second hip-flexor stretch on each side. After dinner, walk for two minutes with your arms swinging behind you instead of scrolling.

You are not trying to make your stomach disappear. You are giving your middle some space to stop bracing.

If your evening routine only changes what you eat, you may be ignoring the position you spent the whole day in.
