## Script

Why your hip feels stuck when you get out of the car

You step out of the car after a normal drive, straighten up, and one hip feels like it needs a minute to join the rest of you. The first few steps are short, awkward, and sometimes you catch yourself standing with more weight on the other leg.

That feeling does not automatically mean something is damaged. A car seat holds your hips bent, your trunk fairly still, and your feet in a very small range for a long time. Your hip joint, glutes, and the muscles across the front of the hip have spent that whole drive doing one job: keeping you parked. Then the moment you stand, they are asked to extend, stabilize, and move your leg behind you again.

You may notice it most when getting out of a low car, climbing the first curb, putting on a shoe, or taking the stairs to your front door. One side can feel more stubborn if that is the leg you use most to brake, carry bags, or lean on while standing.

Try a small reset before you rush away. While still seated, slide to the edge of the seat and place both feet under your knees. Shift a little weight from one sitting bone to the other for ten seconds. Stand up without twisting, then take a slow 30-second walk before you grab your bags. If it feels comfortable, use a wall or the car for balance and let one leg rest behind you in a gentle split stance for 20 seconds, then switch.

The goal is not to stretch your hip as hard as possible. It is to give it a few different jobs before you ask it to carry your whole day. Persistent pain, a fall, or a hip that suddenly will not bear weight deserves medical care.
