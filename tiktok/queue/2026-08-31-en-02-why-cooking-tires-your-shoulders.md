Why cooking dinner can make your shoulders feel tired

If your shoulders feel tired by the time dinner is ready, even though you never lifted anything heavy, your arms may have spent the whole evening working slightly in front of you.

Think about what cooking asks for. You chop with your elbows away from your sides. You stir at a counter that may be a little too low. You reach into cupboards, hold a pan steady, look down at the cutting board, then turn back to the stove. None of those jobs is hard on its own.

The sneaky part is that your shoulder blades and upper back have to keep your arms organized while you repeat them. When your head drifts forward and your ribs stay still, the small muscles around your neck often join the job. That is when a normal dinner can leave you rubbing the tops of your shoulders like you just carried boxes.

More stretching is not always the first thing your body wants. Give it some movement instead. Between steps, let your arms hang and take three slow breaths that move your ribs. Reach both arms overhead once, then let them come down without shrugging. After dinner, walk for two minutes with an easy arm swing. The point is to stop asking the same small muscles to hold the same position all evening.

If shoulder pain is sharp, persistent, follows a fall, or limits normal use of your arm, it deserves an in-person assessment.
