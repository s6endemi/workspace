# Why your stomach feels tight by evening even when you barely ate

- Format: symptom
- Experiment: PREVIA bridge placed after the symptom cascade, before the fixes

## Script

Why your stomach feels tight by evening even when you barely ate

By evening, your jeans feel tighter, your belly feels braced, and taking a deep breath seems weirdly difficult. You barely ate differently. So why does your middle feel like it has been switched on all day?

Sometimes the answer is not your stomach. It is the position you have been holding around it. Hours of sitting, leaning toward a screen, or holding tension during focused work can leave the front of the body shortened and the rib cage moving less. Your diaphragm still has to work under the ribs. If it gets only small, upper chest breaths all afternoon, the whole area can feel guarded by dinner time.

That does not explain every kind of bloating. Food, digestion, hormones, and medical conditions matter too. But there is a useful pattern to notice: you feel more comfortable after a walk, you unconsciously loosen your waistband when you stand up, or your first deep breath after work feels much easier than the one you took at your desk.

PREVIA Health looks for movement patterns and compensations in a five minute phone based scan. It cannot explain digestive symptoms, but it can show when the way you move has become unusually restricted.

Try this before dinner. Stand with your hands around the lower sides of your ribs. Take six slow breaths and try to expand your hands sideways, not just lift your chest. Then take a two minute walk, allowing your arms to swing. Finish with five gentle reaches overhead, exhaling as your arms come down.

You are not trying to pull your stomach flat. You are giving your ribs and abdomen room to move again. If bloating is severe, new, persistent, or comes with pain, vomiting, bleeding, weight loss, or bowel changes, speak to a clinician.

## Redakteur-Prompt

Create a German explanatory video, 60 to 90 seconds maximum, never longer. Use a warm, curious Kurzgesagt-style narration with a relatable evening scene: someone loosens their waistband and cannot get a satisfying deep breath even though they did not eat much. Carefully reveal that prolonged sitting and guarded screen posture can make the ribs and diaphragm move less, which can leave the midsection feeling braced. Do not claim this explains all bloating. Name the alternative context plainly: food, digestion, hormones, and medical conditions can matter. Use concrete self-observations: feeling better after a walk, standing up to loosen the waistband, and an easier first deep breath after work. This batch intentionally places the single casual PREVIA bridge after that cascade and before the fixes. Then show six sideways rib breaths, a two minute walk, and five easy overhead reaches. Do not frame this as treatment or a cure. End with clear red-flag guidance for severe, new, persistent symptoms or pain, vomiting, bleeding, weight loss, or bowel changes. Every sentence must earn its place.
