## English source script

Your stride did not get old. It got smaller.

You can walk every day and still feel stiff after a normal trip to the shop. That sounds backwards until you watch what happens to a stride when life gets busy. The steps do not disappear. They get smaller, quicker, and more cautious. Your feet land under you, but the back leg does less of the work.

That matters because walking is not just a step counter. A comfortable stride asks your ankle to roll, your hip to move behind you, and your arms to counter the motion. When every walk becomes a rush from desk to car to supermarket, you can collect plenty of steps without giving that sequence much practice.

With PREVIA, you can check your walking pattern instead of guessing. Look for simple differences: does one leg push you forward less, does one arm barely swing, or does your body rotate more on one side? It is a fast way to turn a vague feeling of stiffness into something you can actually see.

On your next short walk, do not force a giant stride. For ten relaxed steps, let the back foot stay on the ground a fraction longer. Let the heel lift naturally. Then let your opposite arm swing behind you rather than keeping both hands glued to your pockets. Keep the pace easy enough that you could talk.

The goal is not a perfect gait. It is to remind your body that walking has more options than shuffling between appointments. After those ten steps, return to your normal walk and notice what changed. Many people feel the difference most in the first few minutes, when the hips and ankles finally stop acting like they are still under a desk.

PREVIA can show you the pattern. Your daily walk is where you get to rehearse a different one.
