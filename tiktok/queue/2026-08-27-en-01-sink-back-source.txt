Why your lower back feels tired at the sink

Your lower back can feel more tired doing the dishes than it did sitting at your desk all day.

That sounds backwards until you look at what happens at the sink. The counter is a little too low or a little too far away. You stand still, lean forward just enough to reach the plates, and your hips barely move. Your lower-back muscles stay on quietly to keep you from folding over. Ten minutes of that can feel longer than a walk because there is no change of position.

Your back is not necessarily weak. It may simply be doing the same small job without a break while your hips and legs stay out of the conversation.

You may notice the pattern in other places too: brushing your teeth, cooking at the counter, waiting in a line, even holding a child while you look down. The ache often builds when you are almost still, then eases once you start walking around.

Try a small reset next time. Put one foot on the open cabinet ledge or a low step, then switch sides after a minute. Keep a soft bend in both knees instead of locking them. Every few dishes, shift your weight slowly from one foot to the other and let your hips move with it. These are not exercises. They are ways to stop one small patch of your back from doing all the standing.

If back pain is sharp, follows an injury, or keeps getting worse, get it checked by a qualified clinician. For the ordinary sink ache, position changes are often a useful place to start.
