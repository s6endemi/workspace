## Skript (EN, NotebookLM-Quelle)

Why uneven pavement feels harder after a desk day

A cracked pavement tile should not feel like a surprise obstacle. But after a long day at a desk, your feet can act like they have forgotten their small job: adjusting to the ground before the rest of you has to react.

You step off a curb, your ankle wobbles, and suddenly your whole leg grabs for stability. The instinct is to blame weak ankles. Often the more useful question is simpler: has your foot had any reason to adapt today?

Sitting gives your feet almost no changing surface, no shifts of weight, no chance to spread, press, and guide your balance. Then the walk home asks for all of it at once. Your toes stay rigid, your ankle gets late to the movement, and your knee or hip may take over the correction.

With PREVIA, you can test your movement pattern on your phone and see where one part of the chain starts doing extra work. It is a useful reality check before you decide the problem is only at your ankle.

Try this before your next walk. Stand barefoot near a wall or chair. Slowly shift your weight from heel to forefoot without gripping the floor with your toes. Then make three slow circles with each ankle in both directions. Finish with ten quiet weight shifts from one foot to the other, keeping the whole foot in contact with the ground.

This is not a fix for an injury. It is a small way to give your feet some movement information before the uneven ground does the teaching for you. Notice whether the first few steps feel less clumsy. That difference is worth paying attention to.
