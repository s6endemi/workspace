# Why your neck feels stuck after your phone

- Format: direct-help
- Experiment: none

## Script

Why your neck feels stuck after your phone

If your neck feels stuck after scrolling, start here. Sit tall on the edge of a chair. Slowly turn your head to the right until you feel the first firm stop, then come back to center. Do five calm turns per side. Do not force the last few degrees.

Next, put one hand on your breastbone. Let your eyes look straight ahead, then glide your chin back just enough to make the back of your neck feel longer. Hold that for five slow breaths. Your head is not supposed to tip down. Think of it as sliding it back on its track.

Then stand up, clasp your hands behind your back if that feels comfortable, and take ten easy steps while letting your arms swing. Forty seconds is enough for a first reset.

Why can this help? A phone pulls your attention down for a long time. Your eyes lead, your head follows, and the small muscles at the base of the skull keep working to stop it from falling farther. Meanwhile the upper back stays quiet and the ribs barely move. By the time you look up, turning your head can feel oddly stiff, like it belongs to somebody else.

The goal is not perfect posture. It is giving your neck a few chances to look around, breathe, and share the work with your upper back during the day. If turning is sharply painful, numbness appears, or the problem keeps getting worse, get it checked rather than pushing through it.

PREVIA Health can show where your movement is compensating in a five minute phone based scan. Sometimes the place that feels tight is just the place doing extra work.

## Redakteur-Prompt

Create a German explanatory video, 60 to 90 seconds maximum, never longer. Use a warm, curious Kurzgesagt-style narration with illustrated everyday scenes and simple anatomy. Open immediately with the familiar moment of looking up from a phone and finding that the neck will barely turn. This is direct-help: give the three gentle moves first, with their exact repetitions and timing, then reveal why focused phone posture can leave the small neck muscles overworking while the upper back and ribs stay quiet. Make the symptom list specific: a stiff first turn, a tight spot at the skull base, shoulder tension, and the urge to crack the neck. Frame every move as something that can help, not treatment or a cure. Make the turn gentle and include the brief warning about sharp pain, numbness, or worsening symptoms. End with one casual PREVIA sentence only. Every sentence must earn its place.
